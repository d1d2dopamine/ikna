Ikna is Anki turned around. The cards are already written; the app decides how few of them you see today.

It was built for an ADHD brain, which is a polite way of saying it was built by one. Every feature that makes a review app feel like a debt collector has been left out on purpose:

* No "add card" button. There never will be one. Making cards is the procrastination, not the studying.
* No streaks. Missing a day costs nothing, because a broken streak is a reason to quit, not a reason to return.
* No visible backlog. Skip a week and nothing accuses you of anything; the load governor quietly spreads the work back out.
* One card is a finished day. The daily minimum is one, and it counts.

What it actually does:

* Chunks, not single words. You learn a phrase in context, then the same chunk comes back as recognition, as a gap to fill, and finally as something you produce yourself.
* FSRS-4.5 scheduling, the same algorithm modern Anki uses, with a load governor on top that looks at what is due, what is coming, and how fast you answer, then decides how much today can hold.
* Swipe, do not aim. Left is "not yet", right is "got it". Tap to reveal. There are no small buttons to hit.
* Nothing leaves the phone. No account, no sync, no analytics, no network permission at all. Speech uses the engine already installed on your device.
* Your history is yours: the review log is exported as plain JSONL every week, and can be restored into a fresh install.
* Russian, English and Polish interface. English and Polish learning packs are included.

Open source, GPL-3.0-or-later.
